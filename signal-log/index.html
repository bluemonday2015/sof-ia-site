Logged 9 Oct 2026

AI-designed proteins carried a hidden watermark and still worked

Google DeepMind's SynthID Bio embeds a detectable signature in AI-designed protein sequences. In lab tests against three target proteins, including one from SARS-CoV-2, watermarked binders matched unwatermarked ones on binding affinity and hit rate, with near-perfect detection. The authors call it a proof of concept.

Knowing where a protein design came from could help DNA synthesis providers screen orders.

A machine-checked proof of the Unique Games Conjecture

OpenAI released 722 manuscripts from an internal model, grouped into 372 result families. Among them is a proof of Subhash Khot's Unique Games Conjecture, verified in the Lean proof assistant. Only about 42% of the headline results are formalised; the rest await checking, and three were withdrawn within a day.

It pins down exactly how well problems like Max-Cut can ever be approximated.

An AI found the first real speed-up for two classic computing problems

Claude, an Anthropic model, discovered an algorithm that beats the textbook methods for 3SUM and All-Pairs Shortest Paths, refuting two long-standing hardness hypotheses. Josh Alman and Virginia Vassilevska Williams simplified and extended it and wrote the paper. Its main results are machine-checked in Lean.

A barrier researchers had treated as fundamental turned out not to be.

A light-based detector screened 15 videos at once for deepfakes

UCLA researchers paired a small digital front-end with an optical processor that analyses 15 video streams in a single pass of light. On the Celeb-DF benchmark it identified deepfakes with 97.79% accuracy, and the authors report it held up against compression, noise and adversarial attacks.

Screening has to get cheaper as fake video gets easier to make.
